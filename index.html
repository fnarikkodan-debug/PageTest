01 / TC

Reading timecode

Every frame has a timecode address. Four pairs of digits count from hours down to single frames. The separator before the frames also tells you how the frames are counted.

01:04:32;17
HHHours. Reel 1 usually starts at 01.
MMMinutes, 00–59
SSSeconds, 00–59
FFFrames, 00 up to the frame rate minus one

; before the frames means drop-frame: frame numbers 00 and 01 are skipped every minute, except every tenth minute, so the clock stays in sync with 29.97 fps. : means non-drop-frame, where every frame number is used.

25PAL broadcast · NDF · FF 00–24
29.97NTSC broadcast · DF ; · FF 00–29
59.94High frame rate · DF ; · FF 00–59

04 / EDL

Decoding an EDL

05 / SIZE

Shot-size shorthand

Scripts, shot lists and bin labels use the same short codes for how much of the subject is in frame. Here they run from widest to tightest, with the same turkey framed at each size.

ELSExtreme long shot. The setting dominates.
LSLong shot. The whole body is in frame.
MLSMedium long shot. Knees or legs up.
MSMedium shot. Waist up, or wings up for a turkey.
MCUMedium close-up. Chest and head.
CUClose-up. The head fills the frame.
ECUExtreme close-up. A single detail, such as the eye.

06 / JKL

Hands on the keys

Editors write these keys in notes, such as "J-K-L to the strut, then I/O". Nearly every editing app uses this layout.

J◀◀Play in reverse. Tap again to go faster.
K❚❚Pause. Hold it with J or L to step frame by frame.
L▶▶Play forward. Tap again to go faster.
I[ INMark the first frame you want.
OOUT ]Mark the last frame you want.
